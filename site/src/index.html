Watchdog is a desktop app for journalists who work with public records. Drop in court filings, corporate registries, land titles and freedom-of-information releases. It reads every page, pulls out the people, companies, addresses and relationships, and ties every fact to the page it came from.

How it works

What happens when you add a document.

Every document goes through three stages. The first runs entirely on your computer. The second sends the document's text to the AI model you chose. The third combines both, and code checks every citation the model writes.

  1. 1 On your computer

    Pre-processing

    Each file is turned into text you can search. Docling reads the layout and tables, scanned pages go through OCR one at a time, and Whisper transcribes recordings. Nothing is sent anywhere.

  2. 2 The AI model you choose

    Processing

    A model works out what kind of record it is, then reads the whole document with a guide for that kind, noting each fact and its page. A small model on your computer, GLiNER, first lists the names and figures on each page, so one buried in a table is less likely to be missed.

  3. 3 Both

    Post-processing

    Watchdog gathers every fact about each person, company and place into one note. A model decides which names refer to the same entity, flags contradictions and writes a briefing. Code then checks that every citation points at a real fact.

Contradictions

Two official records. Two different prices.

Watchdog compares each new document with what the investigation already holds and flags any disagreement, citing both sources. Working out which record is right is still your job.

Checking facts

Every fact comes with its source.

AI makes mistakes, so Watchdog shows its work. Each fact carries its page and the passage it rests on. Facts the model inferred rather than read are marked, and code checks every number against the page. Then you record your own check.

  • Source passages are found by ordinary code, not AI
  • Figures that don't appear on the cited page are flagged
  • Your checks follow the fact wherever it appears

Public records only

What stays on your computer, and what doesn't.

Your original files never leave your computer. The text of each document does go to the AI model you choose, and that can't be taken back. That's why Watchdog is only for documents that are public, or presumptively public, and why it asks you to confirm that before it sends anything.

Stays on your computer

  • Your original files
  • Conversion, OCR and transcription
  • The search index
  • Your notes and your checks of each fact
  • The investigation's folder of plain files

Goes to the model you choose

  • Each document's extracted text
  • Your investigation's brief
  • Facts, names and short quotations, for the write-up
  • Your questions to Claude

Never use Watchdog for confidential source communications, leaked or unpublished material, private correspondence, or anything that could identify a source. With a model on your own computer, nothing leaves it at all; see local models.

Questions

Before you start.

Anything else is in the documentation, or ask on GitHub.

What does it cost?

The app is free and open source. You pay for the AI model. A Claude Pro subscription (US$20 a month) is enough for most journalism work. On a metered API key, the default model costs roughly US$1 per 1,000 pages. Controlling cost

Can I use it on leaked or confidential documents?

No. The text of each document is sent to a cloud AI model, so Watchdog is only for records that are public or presumptively public. The one exception is a model running on your own computer, where nothing leaves it. Local models

Does it replace reading the documents?

No. Think of it as a structured first read: it tells you where to look and keeps track of what you've checked. Every fact links to its page. Follow the link before you publish anything. Checking facts

What kinds of files can it read?

PDFs (scanned or not), Word, Excel and PowerPoint files, images, web pages, plain text, and audio and video, which it transcribes on your computer. Supported file types

Where does my work live?

In an ordinary folder of Markdown files on your computer, one per investigation. You own it, and it stays readable without the app. It also opens in Obsidian. Your files

Do I need Claude?

For asking questions of your investigation, yes: Ask Claude and Web research run on Claude. Reading the documents can run on OpenAI, Google Gemini, DeepSeek, OpenRouter or a local model instead. Other providers
